Dishwasher Care Guide (sample reference excerpt)

Filter cleaning. Most dishwashers have a removable filter assembly at the
bottom of the tub, beneath the lower spray arm. Twist it a quarter-turn to
release it, lift it out, and rinse it under running water to remove trapped
food particles. Clean the filter every two to four weeks under normal use;
clean it weekly if you notice food residue on dishes after a cycle. A
clogged filter is the most common cause of dishes coming out gritty or with
a lingering odor.

Spray arm maintenance. The upper and lower spray arms have small holes that
can become blocked by mineral deposits or food debris. Remove each arm
according to the manufacturer's release mechanism (usually a clip or screw
cap) and clear blocked holes with a toothpick or a length of thin wire.
Inspect the spray arms every one to two months.

Descaling. Hard water leaves mineral buildup (limescale) on internal
components over time, which can reduce cleaning performance and shorten the
appliance's life. Run an empty cycle with a dishwasher-safe descaling
solution or white vinegar placed in a cup on the top rack every one to three
months, depending on local water hardness.

Door seal and gasket. Wipe down the rubber door seal monthly with a damp
cloth to prevent mold and mildew buildup, especially in humid climates.
Check for cracks or tears in the gasket at least twice a year — a damaged
seal causes leaks and should be replaced promptly.

Detergent dispenser. If the dispenser door doesn't open fully during a
cycle, buildup around the latch mechanism is often the cause. Clean it with
a damp cloth and avoid overfilling the dispenser, which can leave residue
that prevents the door from releasing cleanly.

Warning signs requiring professional service: standing water at the bottom
of the tub after a completed cycle, unusual grinding or grating noises
during operation, or a persistent burning smell. Do not continue using the
appliance if any of these occur — disconnect power and schedule a repair.
